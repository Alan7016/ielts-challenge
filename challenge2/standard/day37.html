Mini Reading Test — Why We Forget — and How to Remember

A short check-up test: 10 questions, about 15 minutes.

Why We Forget — and How to Remember

Mini reading test · Psychology · 237 words

AIn 1885, the German psychologist Hermann Ebbinghaus tested his own memory. He learned lists of nonsense words, such as ‘WID’ and ‘ZOF’, and then checked how many he could remember later. He found that he forgot more than half of a list within an hour, and about two-thirds after one day. After that, forgetting became slower. He showed his results in a graph that is now called the ‘forgetting curve’.

BEbbinghaus also discovered that reviewing helps. Each time he reviewed a list, he forgot it more slowly. Today, this idea is called ‘spaced repetition’: reviewing information several times, with longer gaps between each review — for example, after one day, then after three days, then after a week.

CMany students do the opposite. They read their notes again and again the night before an exam. This is called ‘cramming’. It can feel useful, because the information starts to seem familiar, but research shows that most of it is forgotten within a few days.

DAnother useful method is testing yourself. In a study at Washington University in 2006, students who read a text and then took a practice test remembered more one week later than students who simply read the text four times. Psychologists call this the ‘testing effect’.

ESleep is important too. While we sleep, the brain moves new memories into long-term storage. So students who stay awake all night to study may remember less, not more.
