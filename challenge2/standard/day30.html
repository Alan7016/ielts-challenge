The two pie charts compare how students at a college divided their weekly spending in 2005 and 2025.

Overall, food was the largest expense in both years. However, the proportion spent on phone and internet increased dramatically, while spending on books and materials fell sharply.

In 2005, food accounted for just over a third of the budget (35%), and this fell slightly to 30% in 2025. Books and materials made up a fifth of spending in 2005, but by 2025 this figure had dropped to only 5%, or one in twenty. The share spent on transport also decreased, from 15% to 10%.

In contrast, spending on phone and internet rose from just 5% to a quarter of the budget, making it the second largest category in 2025. The proportion spent on other items doubled from 5% to 10%, while entertainment remained unchanged at 20% in both years.

Speaking Part 2 — A Piece of Technology

You have 1 minute to prepare and should speak for 1–2 minutes. Read the model answer first and notice how it covers every point on the card.

Part 2 task card

Describe a piece of technology that you use every day.

You should say:
  what it is
  when you got it
  what you use it for
and explain how your life would be different without it.

Model answer

I’d like to talk about my laptop. It’s quite an old one — I got it about three years ago as a present from my parents when I started preparing for university exams.

I use it every single day. In the mornings I usually do online lessons and practice tests on it, and in the evenings I watch films or talk to my cousins who live abroad. I also use it to type my essays, because it’s much faster than writing by hand.

If I didn’t have it, my life would be much more difficult. I would have to go to an internet café to study, which takes time and money, and I wouldn’t be able to see my cousins’ faces when we talk. So although it’s a bit slow now, I really can’t imagine my day without it.
