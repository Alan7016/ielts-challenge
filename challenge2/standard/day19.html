Article — The Bees on the Hospital Roof

A short article that brings this week together: bees (Days 14–15), numbers and trends (Day 17), and opinions (Day 18).

The Bees on the Hospital Roof

City life · 272 words

In 2019, staff at a large hospital in Manchester put two beehives on the roof. At first, some patients were worried. "I thought the bees would come into the building," said one. But the bees stayed outside, and today they are one of the most popular things about the hospital.

The number of hives has grown every year. There were two in 2019, five in 2021 and twelve in 2024. The amount of honey has also increased. In the first year, the bees made approximately 20 kilograms. In 2024, they made about 150 kilograms, which the hospital sells in its café. All the money goes to a children's charity.

Why put bees on a hospital? The hospital's head gardener, Ruth Ellis, gives three reasons. Firstly, bees are important for nature: they carry pollen from flower to flower, so plants can grow. Secondly, the hives help patients relax. Every Thursday, small groups of patients can watch the bees through a glass window. "Many people say it is the best part of their week," Ruth says. Thirdly, the project teaches visitors about the environment.

However, not everyone agrees that city bees are a good idea. Some scientists say there are now too many hives in some cities, and not enough flowers. If there are too many honeybees, they might take food from wild bees, which are already endangered. For this reason, the hospital has also planted 3,000 flowers on its roof and in its gardens.

Ruth believes other hospitals should copy the idea, but carefully. "You must plant flowers first," she says. "If you only add hives, you will not help nature at all."
