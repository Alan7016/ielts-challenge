Board Games Are Back
In an age of smartphones, an old-fashioned hobby is enjoying a surprising return.

Catch-up reading · Free time · 364 words

ATwenty years ago, many people thought that board games would soon disappear. Children had video games, adults had smartphones, and the old boxes of Monopoly and chess were left in cupboards. But something surprising has happened. Sales of board games have grown every year for more than a decade, and in many cities there are now ‘board game cafés’, where customers pay a small fee to play games from a library of hundreds.

BThe first board game café in North America, Snakes & Lattes, opened in Toronto in 2010. Its owners were not sure that anyone would come. Within a few months, however, there were queues outside the door at weekends, and the café soon had to move to a larger building. Today, there are thousands of similar cafés around the world, from London to Seoul.

CWhy are people returning to board games? One reason is that they offer a break from screens. Many people spend all day looking at computers and phones, and a board game is a chance to sit around a table and talk face to face. Another reason is that modern games are very different from the old ones. Instead of simply rolling dice and moving around a board, players often have to make plans, work together and solve problems.

DBoard games may also be good for us. Some studies suggest that older people who play games regularly have a lower risk of memory problems, although scientists say more research is needed. For children, games can teach useful skills, such as counting, taking turns and learning how to lose without getting angry.

EThe internet, surprisingly, has helped too. Websites allow small companies to raise money for new games directly from players, and video reviews help people choose which games to buy. In 2023, one popular game raised more than two million dollars online before it was even made.

FNot everyone is convinced that the trend will last. Some people say board games are simply fashionable at the moment, like many hobbies before them. But for the many families and friends who now spend their Friday evenings around a table, the old-fashioned board game seems to have found a new life.

The line graph compares the average number of weekly visitors to a board game café on weekdays and at weekends over a seven-year period from 2018 to 2024.

Overall, visitor numbers increased considerably over the period, despite a sharp drop in 2020. Weekends were always much busier than weekdays.

In 2018, the café had about 300 visitors at weekends and 120 on weekdays. Both figures rose slightly in 2019, but in 2020 they fell sharply, to 180 and 90 respectively, the lowest points on the graph.

After that, both lines rose steadily. Weekend visitors increased to 450 in 2022 and reached a peak of 520 in 2024. Weekday numbers also grew, from 160 in 2021 to 300 in 2024, which was more than three times the 2020 figure.
