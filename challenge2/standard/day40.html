Bees on the Roof

Recap reading · Nature in the city · 182 words

AOn the roof of the Paris Opera House, more than thirty metres above the busy streets, there are beehives. They have been there since the 1980s, and the honey is sold in a shop nearby. Today, thousands of hives can be found on roofs, balconies and gardens in cities such as London, New York and Paris.

BMany people start city beekeeping because they want to help bees. Cities can be surprisingly good places for bees: parks, gardens and trees offer many different flowers, and farmers’ pesticides are not used.

CHowever, some scientists now warn that there may be too many honeybees in some cities. Honeybees and wild bees eat the same food, so when there are many hives, there may not be enough flowers for wild bees. In a 2019 study in Paris, researchers found that wild bees were less common in areas with many hives.

DExperts say that the best way to help bees in cities is not to add more hives but to plant more flowers. Even a small window box with the right plants can give food to bees.

The bar chart compares the average number of hours that people in four age groups slept each night in 2005 and 2025.

Overall, every age group slept less in 2025 than in 2005, and the youngest group slept the most in both years.

In 2005, 12–15-year-olds slept for 9 hours a night on average, compared with 8.5 hours for 16–18-year-olds. By 2025, these figures had fallen to 8 hours and 7 hours respectively, so the 16–18 group saw the biggest decrease, of one and a half hours.

The older groups saw smaller changes. People aged 19–25 slept 8 hours in 2005 but only 7 hours in 2025, while the figure for 26–40-year-olds fell only slightly, from 7.5 to 7 hours. As a result, in 2025 the three oldest groups all slept for the same amount of time.
