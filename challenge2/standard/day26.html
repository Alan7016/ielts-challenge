Walking Football: The Slow Game

Catch-up passage · Sport · 273 words

AFootball is usually a game for the young and fast. But in sports halls and parks across Britain, and now in many other countries, a different version is becoming popular. It is called walking football, and the most important rule is simple: nobody is allowed to run. If a player runs, the referee gives a free kick to the other team.

BThe game was first played in England in 2011. It was designed for men and women over fifty who loved football but could no longer play the normal game because of their age or injuries. Other rules make the game safer as well. Players may not kick the ball above head height, and hard tackles are not allowed.

CAt first, some people laughed at the idea. However, the game has grown quickly. There are now thousands of players, local leagues and even international matches between national teams of older players. Some clubs report that their oldest players are over eighty.

DDoctors say that the benefits are not only physical. Walking football improves balance and fitness, but many players say that the best part is the social side. For people who have retired or who live alone, a weekly match is a chance to make friends and feel part of a team again. After the game, most players stay for a cup of tea and a chat.

EWalking football is not easy, though. Players who are used to the normal game often find it very difficult not to run, especially when the ball is near the goal. As one coach put it, the hardest skill in walking football is controlling your own legs.
