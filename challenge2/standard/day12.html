Article — Smarter Public Transport

Read the article below, then answer the questions that follow.

Cities around the world are using new technology to make public transport more efficient. Sensors on buses and trains now track exactly where each vehicle is, so apps can tell passengers precisely when the next bus will arrive, rather than relying on a fixed timetable that traffic jams can easily disrupt.

Payment has also become more convenient. In many cities, passengers can simply tap a bank card instead of buying a paper ticket, which speeds up boarding considerably and reduces queues at rush hour. Some transport systems even adjust ticket prices automatically, charging less during off-peak hours to encourage people to travel outside the busiest times.

Not every change has been welcomed. Older passengers, in particular, sometimes find new apps and payment systems confusing, and cities have had to keep traditional ticket machines available for anyone who prefers them. Even so, most transport authorities believe that smarter, technology-based systems are the clearest way to get more people out of their cars and onto public transport.

True or false?

1. Sensors help apps show passengers when the next bus will arrive.

2. In many cities, passengers can now pay by tapping a bank card.

3. Ticket prices are always exactly the same, no matter the time of day.

4. Every single passenger has found the new technology easy to use.

5. Traditional ticket machines have been kept available in some cities.
