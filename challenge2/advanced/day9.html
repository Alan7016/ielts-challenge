Task 2 of 6

Article — Why More People Are Learning English Than Ever Before

Read the article below, then answer the questions that follow.

Of the thousands of languages spoken around the world, none has spread quite like English. It is the most widely studied second language on the planet, and the number of people learning it shows no sign of slowing down. So why has interest in English grown so steadily, and in some regions so dramatically, over the past few decades?

Part of the answer is globalisation. As trade, travel, and communication between countries have increased, English has become the language most likely to be shared by two people who don't speak the same native language. A businessperson in Seoul and a businessperson in São Paulo may well conduct their meeting in English, even though neither grew up speaking it.

The internet has accelerated this trend even further. A huge proportion of online content — from academic research to entertainment — is published in English, giving learners a constant, informal reason to keep studying: to read, watch, and understand things they're already interested in. Universities have played a role too, with a growing number of degree programmes worldwide now taught partly or entirely in English, even in countries where it isn't an official language.

This doesn't mean becoming a native-level speaker is the goal for everyone. Many learners are simply aiming to become proficient enough to work, study, or travel comfortably — and employers increasingly value multilingual staff who can operate in more than one language, even imperfectly, over monolingual staff who can only work in one.

None of this suggests other languages are becoming less important; demand for languages like Mandarin and Spanish has also grown in specific regions and industries. But for now, at least, English remains the closest thing the world has to a shared second language.

Useful language for describing trends (Writing Task 1)

You'll use phrases like these throughout today's Writing Task 1 work. Here are a few to start with:

  • rose steadily — went up gradually, over a period of time
  • increased dramatically — went up suddenly, by a large amount
  • remained stable — stayed roughly the same
  • fluctuated — went up and down irregularly
  • reached a peak — hit its highest point
  • fell sharply — went down suddenly, by a large amount
  • declined gradually — went down slowly, over time
