A single journalist can now reach a huge audience by choosing the right platform to publish on. A story doesn't need a large network of TV stations behind it to go viral — a single controversial post can influence millions within hours. This makes it more important than ever to check whether a source is genuinely credible, since some governments still try to censor information they don't want the public to see.

Vocabulary — Cause, Factor, and Reason

IELTS candidates often confuse cause, factor, and reason. Complete these sentences by writing one of the words above in each gap.

1. Another key in the process has been the mobile phone.

2. I think the main of this change has been the internet.

3. Newspapers only print a few thousand of the letters they receive each day, and the for this is that they just don't have enough space.

Check your answers by reading these definitions. Which words are often used before and after cause, factor, and reason?

cause — the reason why something, especially something bad, happens: The police are still trying to establish the cause of the fire.

factor — a fact or situation that influences the result of something: Price will be a major/crucial factor in the success of this new product.

reason — the cause of an event or situation or something which provides an excuse or explanation: The reason for the disaster was engine failure, not human error. The reason why grass is green was a mystery to the little boy.

Notice: a major/crucial factor; the cause of something; the reason for/why something.

Look at this Part 3 question and the three answers below. Which answer do you think is best? Why?

How does the Internet help people in their everyday lives?

A — I use the Internet for downloading films. I enjoy watching films in my free time, and it's cheaper than going to the cinema.

B — Well, I think it helps people in quite a lot of ways, for instance to get information, or to book air tickets, it helps people to study and to do research about their homework and even to get advice about how to study.

C — Yes, I think it helps people a lot.

Read this response given by Elena, an IELTS candidate.

"Well, I'm not sure. Some people do perhaps, for example young people who should be studying instead, or a lot of people use the Internet for their jobs or for other things. Maybe too much time chatting to friends, not enough time doing other things. But many people leave the Internet connected all day because they use it instead of a telephone for messages or instead of going to the library, so I think it depends. For some things, it saves time."

1. Which question from Speaking Part 3 (Exercise 5) do you think Elena is answering?

2. Underline (or note down) the reasons and examples she gives.

3. Which four words or phrases does she use to show she's not entirely certain?

1. Most likely: "Do you think that some people spend too much time on the Internet? Why?" — her answer directly addresses whether people spend too much time online.

2. Examples: young people who should be studying, chatting to friends instead of doing other things, using it instead of a telephone or the library. Reason: "it depends" — sometimes it saves time, sometimes it wastes it.

3. "I'm not sure", "perhaps", "maybe", "I think" (also "some people do").

Pronunciation note — chunking: notice how a fluent speaker groups words into short, natural phrases when speaking, pausing briefly between chunks rather than word by word — this makes speech sound more natural and easier to follow.
