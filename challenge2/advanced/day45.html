Reading Recap — The Last Few Days Before the Exam

A short reading with the question types you know well — about 12 minutes, 8 questions.

The Last Few Days Before the Exam

Recap reading · Exam preparation

AIn the final days before an important exam, many students try to study as much as possible, often staying up late to review everything one last time. Research on memory suggests that this is rarely the best strategy. New information is easily forgotten unless it is reviewed several times, with gaps between each review, and the brain needs sleep to store what has been learned.

BExperts therefore recommend that the last week should be used mainly for practice under exam conditions rather than for learning new material. Completing full practice tests with a timer helps students get used to the pace of the exam, and checking the answers afterwards shows which question types still cause problems.

CNerves are normal. A little anxiety can actually improve performance by keeping people alert, but too much can make it hard to concentrate. Simple techniques, such as slow breathing and preparing everything the night before, can help. Many teachers also advise students to avoid comparing themselves with friends in the final days, since this often increases stress.

DOn the day itself, small decisions make a difference: eating breakfast, arriving early and reading every instruction carefully. Above all, experts say, students should remember that the exam measures what they have built over weeks and months — not what they do in the last few hours.

Introduction: With the rapid growth of online learning, some people predict that traditional classrooms will soon disappear. Although online courses offer clear advantages, I disagree that they will replace classroom teaching entirely.

Body paragraph: Online courses are undeniably flexible and affordable: learners can study at any time and from anywhere, often for a fraction of the cost of a classroom course. This course itself is an example — students completed daily tasks online and received feedback from mentors. However, many learners struggle to stay motivated without a teacher and classmates, and skills such as speaking and teamwork are difficult to develop fully on a screen. For these reasons, online learning is more likely to complement classroom teaching than to replace it.
