Writing Task 1 — Your Process Report

Write a full Task 1 report on a process. Spend about 20 minutes and write at least 150 words. Use the passive, a range of sequencers, and an overview that gives the number of stages.

  • Introduction: shows → illustrates; how … are recycled → the process by which … are recycled.
  • Overview: number of stages, first and last stage, and that it is a cycle.
  • Paragraph 3: stages 1–4 (collection to cleaning).
  • Paragraph 4: stages 5–8 (crushing to filling). Vary your sequencers.

Fill in every box above first — then you can compare. Your answers lock when you open this.

The diagram illustrates the process by which used glass bottles are recycled to produce new ones.

Overall, there are eight stages in the process, which begins with the collection of used bottles and ends with new bottles being filled and sent to shops. It is therefore a cycle in which glass can be reused repeatedly.

First, used bottles are left in recycling bins, from where they are collected and transported by truck to a recycling plant. At the plant, the bottles are sorted according to their colour. Following this, they are cleaned, and any labels and caps are removed.

Once the bottles have been cleaned, they are crushed into small pieces. These pieces are then heated in a furnace until they melt, after which the liquid glass is moulded into new bottles. Finally, the new bottles are filled and delivered to shops, where they can be bought, used and recycled again.

Task 8 of 10

Speaking Part 2 — Two Practice Cards

Read the model answer and notice the range of tenses and the personal reflection at the end. Then do both cards: 1 minute to prepare, up to 2 minutes to speak.

Part 2 task card

Describe something you did to help the environment.

You should say:
  what you did
  when and where you did it
  who you did it with
and explain how you felt about doing it.

Model answer (Band 7+)

I’d like to describe a clean-up day I took part in last spring. A local volunteer group had organised it along the river near my neighbourhood, which, to be honest, had become pretty neglected — there were plastic bottles and bags caught in the bushes everywhere.

I went with two friends from my course, and there must have been about forty of us altogether. We were given gloves and bags and spent the whole morning collecting rubbish. By lunchtime we’d filled over a hundred bags, and some of the bottles had obviously been there for years.

As for how I felt, I was exhausted but genuinely proud. What struck me most was how quickly a place can be transformed when people work together. It also changed my own habits — since then I’ve stopped buying bottled water and carry a refillable bottle instead. So I’d say it had a bigger effect on me than on the river!

1. Which features make this a strong Part 2 answer? Choose the best description.
