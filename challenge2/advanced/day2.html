Watch out for these confusable pairs

problem vs trouble — problem is countable ("a problem", "problems"); trouble is usually uncountable and used in fixed phrases like "have trouble doing something" or "get into trouble".

affect vs effect — affect is a verb meaning "to cause a change" ("the weather affects my mood"); effect is a noun meaning the change or result itself ("the effect of the weather").

percent vs percentage — use percent directly after a number ("73 percent"); use percentage with "the" or a general word like "a small/large" ("the percentage of people...", "a small percentage").

Vocabulary — problem or trouble? affect or effect?

IELTS candidates often confuse problem/trouble and affect/effect.

trouble or problem?

Problem means "a situation that causes difficulties and needs to be dealt with." You can talk about a problem or problems. e.g. Tell me what the problem is. He's having a few problems at work.

Trouble means "problems, difficulties or worries" and is used in a more general way. Trouble is almost always uncountable, so don't use "a" before it. e.g. We had some trouble while we were on holiday.

affect or effect?

Affect is a verb meaning "to cause a change." e.g. Pollution seriously affects the environment.

Use the noun effect to talk about the change, reaction or result caused by something. e.g. Global warming is one of the effects of pollution.
