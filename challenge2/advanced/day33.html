The Return of Vinyl
Streaming was supposed to make physical music obsolete. Instead, the record is back.

Catch-up reading · Culture and technology · 408 words

AIn 2007, sales of vinyl records in the United States fell to fewer than one million copies, and the format seemed destined for museums. Compact discs had replaced it in the 1980s and 1990s, and digital downloads were now replacing CDs. Yet in 2022, for the first time since 1987, Americans bought more vinyl records than CDs — over forty million of them. The revival is one of the most unexpected stories in the modern music industry.

BThe buyers are not, as many people assume, mainly older listeners trying to recapture their youth. Surveys in both the United States and Britain have found that a large proportion of vinyl buyers are under thirty-five, and many of them own a record player that they bought only recently. Some even admit that they rarely play the records at all.

CWhy would a generation raised on streaming choose such an inconvenient format? Part of the answer is that streaming has made music almost invisible. With millions of songs available instantly for a monthly fee, music has become something that plays in the background rather than something people own. A record, by contrast, is a physical object: it has to be chosen, carried home and placed carefully on a turntable. For many buyers, this ritual is the point.

DSound quality is also frequently mentioned, although here the evidence is more complicated. Enthusiasts describe vinyl as ‘warmer’ than digital audio, but in blind listening tests, most people cannot reliably tell the difference, and some of the qualities that listeners enjoy are in fact small imperfections — the faint crackle of dust, for instance — rather than a more accurate reproduction of the original recording.

EThe revival has created practical problems for the industry. Most of the factories that once pressed records were closed decades ago, and the machines that make them are no longer manufactured in large numbers. As a result, artists sometimes wait six months or more for their records to be produced. The raw material, a type of plastic called PVC, has also attracted criticism from environmental groups, prompting some manufacturers to experiment with recycled and plant-based alternatives.

FFor musicians, vinyl offers something streaming rarely does: a meaningful income. A single stream earns an artist a tiny fraction of a cent, whereas a fan who buys a record may pay twenty-five dollars or more. For many smaller bands, selling records at concerts has become an essential part of making a living.

Why Do We Forget Our Dreams?

Extra reading · Science · 263 words

AMost people dream several times every night, yet by the time they have finished breakfast, they usually remember little or nothing of what they experienced. Researchers estimate that we forget around ninety-five per cent of our dreams. Why should something that feels so vivid while it is happening disappear so quickly?

BOne explanation involves a chemical called noradrenaline, which plays an important role in forming memories. During the stage of sleep in which most vivid dreams occur, known as REM sleep, levels of noradrenaline in the brain are extremely low. Without it, the brain may simply fail to store the experience in long-term memory.

CAnother factor is timing. Studies have found that people who wake up during or immediately after a dream are far more likely to remember it than those who sleep on. People who often recall their dreams also tend to wake up more frequently during the night, although these periods are usually too short for them to notice.

DThe content of the dream matters as well. Dreams that are emotional, strange or frightening are remembered more often than ordinary ones, perhaps because emotional experiences are more easily stored in memory in general.

EFor those who would like to remember more of their dreams, sleep researchers offer simple advice. Keeping a notebook beside the bed and writing down anything you remember as soon as you wake — before checking your phone or getting up — can significantly increase recall within a couple of weeks. Even a single word or image can be enough to bring back a surprising amount of detail.
