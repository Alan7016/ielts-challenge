Insurance for the Harvest: The Global Seed Vault

Catch-up passage · Food security · 339 words

ADeep inside a mountain on the Norwegian archipelago of Svalbard, far north of the Arctic Circle, lies a facility designed to survive almost any catastrophe. Opened in 2008, the Svalbard Global Seed Vault holds duplicate samples of seeds from gene banks around the world — many hundreds of thousands of varieties of crops such as wheat, rice, barley and beans. Its purpose is not to replace those national and regional collections but to back them up: should a gene bank be destroyed by war, natural disaster or simple mismanagement, its seeds could be retrieved from Svalbard.

BThe choice of location was deliberate. The surrounding permafrost and thick rock provide natural insulation, so that even if the cooling system failed, the samples would remain frozen for a considerable time. The site is remote, geologically stable and well above sea level. Crucially, the depositing institutions retain ownership of their seeds; the vault acts, in effect, as a safe-deposit box, and only the depositor may withdraw them.

CFor several years the vault’s value was largely theoretical. That changed when an agricultural research centre that had been based near Aleppo was forced to relocate because of the conflict in Syria and could no longer maintain its collection. In 2015, it made the first ever withdrawal, using the duplicates to regenerate its collection elsewhere — and later returned fresh samples to Svalbard. Supporters regarded this as a vindication of the entire project.

DThe vault is not without its critics. Some argue that freezing seeds preserves genetic material but not the living relationship between crops and the farmers who adapt them, generation after generation, to changing conditions; on this view, diversity is best conserved in farmers’ fields rather than in vaults. Others point out that a seed bank is only as useful as the documentation that accompanies its samples. These objections strike me as complementary rather than contradictory: a vault cannot substitute for living agricultural diversity, but nor can farmers’ fields guarantee survival in the face of war. The prudent course is to pursue both.
