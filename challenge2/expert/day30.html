The two maps illustrate how the centre of Hillbury was redeveloped between 2000 and 2025.

Overall, the town centre has been transformed from an area dominated by traffic and industry into a pedestrian-friendly space focused on shopping, leisure and housing. Only the market square remained unchanged.

The most significant change concerns the High Street, which was once open to traffic but has now been pedestrianised. To the north of it, the car park in the north-west has been replaced by a multi-storey car park, while the bus station in the north-east has been converted into a library and offices. The market square between them has stayed exactly as it was.

South of the High Street, the cinema has been demolished to make way for a shopping centre, and the park has been retained but now includes a playground. The most striking transformation is in the south-east, where the factory has disappeared entirely, its site now divided between apartments and a row of cafés.

Speaking Part 2 — Two Practice Cards

Read the Band 8.5 model and notice its structure: a vivid opening, a clear sequence, precise vocabulary and a reflective ending. Then do both cards — 1 minute to prepare, the full 2 minutes to speak.

Part 2 task card

Describe a place in your city that has changed a lot.

You should say:
  where it is
  what it was like before
  how it has changed
and explain how you feel about the changes.

Model answer (Band 8.5)

The place I’d like to talk about is the area around the old textile factory near my grandparents’ house in Tashkent. When I was a child, it was a rather grim, noisy part of town — lorries coming and going all day, a high concrete wall, and hardly anywhere to walk.

About five years ago, the factory closed and the whole site was redeveloped. The main building wasn’t demolished, interestingly; instead, it was converted into a kind of cultural centre with exhibition spaces and a café, and the land around it has been turned into a park with fountains and cycle paths. What used to be a car park is now a small market at weekends.

As for how I feel, my reaction is broadly positive. The area is far greener and more welcoming, and you see families there every evening. That said, I do feel a slight pang of nostalgia — the factory was part of the city’s industrial history, and some of the people who worked there struggled to find new jobs. So I’d say it’s a change for the better, but not one without a cost.

The highest Part 3 scores come from candidates who can hold a position under pressure. For each statement, give your view, concede a point to the other side, then rebut it.

Position: I’m firmly of the view that… → Concession: Granted, … / I accept that… → Rebuttal: But that overlooks the fact that… / Even so, … → Conclusion: So, on balance, …
